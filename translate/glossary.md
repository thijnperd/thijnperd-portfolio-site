# Glossary — Dutch and German

The terms that recur across the case studies and essays, and how each translation renders
them today. This is a record of decisions already in `case-studies-and-essays.nl.md` and
`.de.md`, not a wish list: when a new page is translated, follow the column on the right so
the three languages keep the same vocabulary.

| English | Dutch | German | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| dither | dither (noun), geditherd (adjective) | Dither (noun), gedithert (adjective) | Both keep the English noun; only the participle is inflected. "geditherd" ×10 in NL, "gedithert" ×10 in DE. |
| ordered dither | geordende dither | geordneter Dither / Geordnete Dither | NL uses "geordende dither" ×4. |
| halftone | halftoon | Halbton | "halftoon" ×2 in NL, "Halbton" ×2 in DE. |
| posterisation | posterisatie | Posterisierung | Noun forms only; the verb is avoided. |
| raster / matrix | raster | Raster | DE uses "Raster" ×8 where English says raster or matrix. |
| vault | kluis | Vault | Deliberate split: NL translates it ("kluis" ×19), DE keeps the English term ("Vault" ×19). Do not change one without the other. |
| local-first | local-first | lokal-first / lokal | NL keeps the English compound (×3); DE leans on "lokal" (×14). |

## Rules that are not in the table

- Headings are sentence case in Dutch, noun-capitalised in German, matching the English
  source rather than each language's typographic default.
- Em dashes stay em dashes (—), not en dashes, so the pages read the same in all three.
- Numbers keep the site's English formatting in all languages ("500 notes", "5,000 links");
  the translations do not switch to 5.000 or 500 Notizen.
- Product names (Dither Studio, Cortex, W3Plan, PapersPlease) are never translated.
